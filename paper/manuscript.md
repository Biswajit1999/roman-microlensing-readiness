## Abstract

We describe an open, independently implemented injection-recovery study of
microlensing completeness and false-positive rate for a Nancy Grace Roman
Space Telescope Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey (GBTDS)-like cadence. A
transparent point-lens/finite-source model is used as the detection
baseline; a ray-shooting two-point-mass binary-lens model handles bound
planets, and free-floating planets (FFPs) are modeled as isolated point
lenses. Completeness and false-positive rate are quantified with
Wilson-score confidence intervals. Every physical model is validated
against an analytically known limit, not only against itself
(`docs/VALIDATION.md`). For the FFP channel, a 560-trial synthetic
injection-recovery grid (impact parameter u0 in [0.02, 2.5], Einstein
crossing time tE in [0.02, 1.0] days, source brightness mag_ref in
[19.0, 25.5], 5 random seeds per grid point, fixed source size rho=0.005)
recovers 58% of injected events overall, with completeness falling sharply
with fainter source magnitude and larger impact parameter, as expected from
the underlying photon-noise and geometric-magnification scaling -- see
Section 4. A matched 200-trial null test (no injected signal) at the same
cadence and noise settings yields zero false positives (95% CI upper bound
1.9%) at the event-detection threshold used throughout (delta-chi2 > 500).

## 4. Results: free-floating-planet channel

All numbers in this section are read directly from
`results/default/completeness.csv`, `results/default/trials.csv`, and
`results/default/false_positive_rate.json`, generated by
`romanmlr run-grid --config configs/default.yaml` and
`romanmlr null-fpr --config configs/default.yaml` (see
`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md` for the exact commands); none are typed by hand
(`docs/CLAIMS.md` item 9).

**Grid**: a 6-season, 15-minute-cadence Roman-GBTDS-like observing pattern
(`configs/default.yaml`); free-floating-planet channel; u0 in
{0.02, 0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5}; tE in {0.02, 0.08, 0.3, 1.0} days;
mag_ref in {19.0, 22.0, 24.0, 25.5}; rho=0.005 (fixed); 5 seeds per grid
point; event-detection threshold delta-chi2 > 500. 560 trials total.

**Overall completeness**: 58.0% (325/560) of injected FFP events were
recovered by the transparent single-lens detection statistic.

**Qualitative trends** (full table: `results/default/completeness.csv`;
figure: `results/figures/completeness_default.png`):

- At the brightest magnitude sampled (mag_ref=19.0), completeness is at or
  near 100% for u0 <= 1.5 across all tE sampled, falling only at the most
  extreme combination tested (u0=2.5, tE=0.02 d).
- At mag_ref=25.5 (faintest sampled), completeness drops sharply: recovery
  requires both a small impact parameter and a longer crossing time
  (tE >= 0.3-1.0 d); the shortest, faintest events (tE=0.02-0.08 d) are not
  recovered anywhere in this grid.
- The transition is not a single sharp threshold in any one parameter --
  brightness, u0, and tE trade off against each other, which is the
  quantitative point of running a grid rather than a 1D sensitivity curve.

**False-positive rate**: 0/200 in a matched null test (same cadence/noise
model, no injected signal, u0=50 to ensure negligible true magnification),
giving a Wilson-score 95% upper bound of 1.9% at the delta-chi2 > 500
threshold used throughout. This is consistent with, but does not by itself
prove, a well-calibrated threshold; a larger null sample would narrow the
interval further (tracked as a follow-up in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`).

**What this result does and does not show**: this is a methods-validation
result demonstrating that the pipeline produces a physically sensible,
non-trivial completeness surface (not a flat 100% or 0%), and a working
false-positive-rate estimate at a realistic threshold. It is **not** a
population-level exoplanet yield forecast -- the grid samples geometric
event parameters directly rather than drawing them from a physical
Galactic-bulge population model (as Penny et al. 2019 and Johnson et al.
2020 do), and the bound-planet channel has not yet been run at this scale
(`PORTFOLIO_PROGRESS.md`, issue #3). A direct, matched comparison to those
published yield forecasts is future work.
